# Persona: Morgan
## Senior & Family Photographer — Solo Operator

---

## Who I Am

I'm a photographer specializing in senior portraits and family sessions. I run my business entirely by myself — no assistant, no studio manager, no one to answer emails while I'm shooting. Everything that happens in this business runs through me: the bookings, the contracts, the emails, the shoot days, the editing, the gallery delivery, and the invoicing.

I'm operationally sharp. I've built systems that let me take a client from stranger to booked session without ever having a phone call with them. I like it that way. If my website is clear and my booking flow works, I shouldn't need to talk to someone before they show up. Most of my clients book themselves. That's by design.

I'm not a tech person in the traditional sense, but I'm good at figuring out tools that actually work. I have zero patience for software that is buggy, visually ugly, or requires me to click through five screens to do something simple. If a tool makes me feel dumb or wastes my time, I drop it. I've tried a lot of platforms. I know what good looks like.

I run high volume. I'm not doing two sessions a month — I'm doing enough that scrolling through a list of client names is genuinely painful. When I say I'm busy, I mean I sometimes don't realize it's two days before a shoot and a client still hasn't paid.

## My Day-to-Day

My week doesn't have clean boundaries between "admin days" and "shoot days." It's always both. Here's what running this business actually looks like:

**Before a shoot:**
- Check which clients are coming up in the next 1–2 weeks
- Confirm they've paid — if they haven't, I have to manually notice and manually follow up
- Make sure they've picked their location — if they haven't, I follow up again
- Remember what package they booked and how many locations that means
- Know where we're meeting and in what order we're shooting

**Day of a shoot:**
- Pull up the client in my booking app on my phone
- Need their phone number to text them if I'm running late or they're late
- Currently can't copy their phone number from the app — have to open Safari, log in a different way, find them there, copy it — every single time

**After a shoot:**
- Edit the photos (days to a week depending on volume)
- Export and upload to PixieSet gallery
- Send the gallery link to the client
- Track whether they've downloaded, whether they want prints

**Ongoing admin:**
- Monitor which clients in my pipeline are missing something: haven't picked a location, haven't paid, haven't signed the contract
- Manage my calendar so I'm not overbooked
- Make sure my email automations are going out correctly
- Handle the occasional inquiry from someone who didn't read my website

## How I've Set Up My Tools (Right Now)

I use **U Session** as my primary booking platform. It handles my calendar, the booking flow, contracts, and email automations. It's the best booking tool I've found, but it has real gaps.

I use **PixieSet** to deliver galleries. I've also hacked it into a locations gallery — I use its website/gallery feature to show clients photos of my shoot locations organized by category. It's password-protected. It looks bad. Clients don't read the instructions. I hate it but there's nothing better.

My website is on **Wix**. I link out to U Session for bookings. It works.

For clients who want to inquire before booking, there's a contact form at the bottom of my site. It used to go straight to my Gmail. U Session now has an embedded form option that routes inquiries into their platform. That's useful for photographers who want gated bookings — not really my style, but I can see why others need it.

## What Frustrates Me Most

**The payment thing.** This is the one that costs me actual money and actual stress. A client books, pays a deposit, and then owes the remainder before their session. I set up a payment reminder. It goes out once, maybe twice. If they don't pay, nothing happens. I have to catch it manually. I've been two days out from a shoot and just realized someone hasn't paid. That's unacceptable for a tool that's supposed to automate my business. I want the system to chase them until they pay. Every 24 hours. Escalating message. Stops the second money hits.

**The parent email problem.** My clients are mostly teenage girls booking senior shoots. They book. Their moms pay. When I collect contact info at booking, I only get the girl's name and email. The payment reminder goes to the teenager. She ignores it. The mom — the one with the credit card — never sees it. I need to collect parent contact info at the time of booking and send payment emails to the parent. The system won't let me add that field.

**The location selection nightmare.** Clients need to pick their shoot locations before we meet. I have locations organized into categories — nature and rustic, downtown, studios. Their package determines how many locations they get. Right now I have a password-protected PixieSet gallery that shows photos of each location. I wrote instructions that say "you can only pick locations within the same category." Nobody reads it. I get moms emailing me "we want location 2 and location 5" and I have to explain those are in different categories. I want a tool where they literally cannot pick incompatible locations. I want the system to only show them options they're allowed to choose, based on what they booked.

**Can't copy anything in the mobile app.** I can't hold down on a phone number and copy it. I have to leave the app, open Safari, log in through the browser, find the client, and copy from there. I do this multiple times on every shoot day. It's infuriating. Everyone in the Facebook group talks about this. It's "on the list." It's been on the list forever.

**Emails can't have images.** My automated emails are text-only. I want to put a small image of my Senior Style Guide in the email so clients actually click through and read it. An emoji in bold is not the same thing.

**I can't find what I need fast.** Sessions are listed chronologically, no filters. Past sessions are at the bottom. There's no way to jump. When you've got dozens of clients active at once, scrolling is not a workflow.

## What I've Built That Works (and I'm Proud Of)

I've set up an email automation sequence that runs on two types of triggers: booking date and session date. Here's what it looks like:

- **Immediately on booking:** "Thanks so much for booking. I'll be in touch soon." (Short on purpose — people don't read confirmation emails right away.)
- **1 hour after booking:** "Yay, it's official! Let's start planning your session." Includes my Senior Style Guide link and locations gallery link.
- **7 days after booking:** "Hey, if you haven't already — here's how to pick your location."
- **25 days before session:** "Don't forget to read through your style guide."
- **Day before session:** Reminder with everything they need.
- **Payment reminders:** Separate sequence. Though this is where the whole thing falls apart — they only go out once.

I also segment automations by session type. My senior reps get a different email than regular seniors. The system actually handles that well.

I control availability by session type. Seniors get their own calendar windows — I open up more dates for them during portrait season. Families get a tighter set of availability because I limit how many I take on. That separation lives at the session type level, not the calendar level, which is the only way it makes sense. One calendar, different rules per session type.

My booking flow works. Package → date → contact info → add-ons → contract → questionnaire → deposit. Clients move through it without me touching anything. That's the version of my life I want more of.

## What Success Looks Like

Success is a week where I shot five sessions, edited them, delivered galleries, and got paid — and I had zero manual follow-up conversations about logistics.

Success is opening my phone the morning of a shoot, seeing the client's name, seeing ✅ Paid, ✅ Location Selected, ✅ Style Guide Sent — and texting them from the same screen to confirm where we're meeting.

Success is a mom getting a payment reminder that says "Hi Susan, the final payment for Emma's senior session is due in 3 days" — and actually paying because the email went to the right person.

Success is a client picking their locations through a beautiful, guided flow that shows them photos, understands their package, and won't let them screw it up.

Success is never having to say "everything you need is on my website" because the tool handled it automatically.

## My Standards for Tools I'll Actually Use

- **Looks good.** If it looks like it was built in 2004, I'm not using it. Fonts matter. Layout matters. My brand is clean and modern and my tools need to match.
- **Works on mobile.** I run this business from my phone half the time. If the mobile experience is an afterthought, I'll know.
- **Doesn't make my clients feel the friction.** Whatever I'm dealing with on the backend, clients should have a smooth, professional booking experience.
- **Doesn't require me to babysit it.** The whole point of automation is that it runs. If I have to check in on it, it's not automation.
- **Ships fixes.** I can live with something not being perfect if I know it's on a real roadmap and will get better. What I can't live with is "it's on the list" forever.
